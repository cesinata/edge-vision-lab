# Error analysis (test split, PyTorch float32 model)

Generated by `python scripts/make_error_analysis_doc.py` from `results/eval_torch.json` and `results/error_analysis.json`; those come from `python train/evaluate.py` and `python train/error_analysis.py`. All numbers are `measured (host CPU)`. No images are stored in the repository. The test set is small (58 videos, few scenes), so treat these as observations to check, not conclusions.

What was checked: wrong share by distance bin, by object size (box area), by crop brightness and by crop texture (pixel standard deviation), per scene cluster, and per video. In addition I looked at 32 randomly chosen misclassified test crops locally, as a montage that was not saved.

## Observations

1. **Drone is the weak class.** 363 of 678 test drone crops (53.5%) were wrong: 258 called neither and 105 called bird. Recall for drone is the lowest of the three classes.

2. **Distance matters a lot for drones.** Wrong share of drone crops: 15.8% close (6 videos), 45.1% medium (16), 89.6% distant (10). Birds: 25.4% medium (10 videos), 9.3% distant (only 2 videos, 43 crops). The distant-bird figure rests on two videos and says almost nothing.

3. **Small objects fail more.** Bird and drone crops split into thirds by box area: wrong share 53.6% (smallest third, 226 to 701 px squared), 45.1% (middle), 35.9% (largest). Size and distance overlap, so this is partly the same effect.

4. **Whole clips fail, not odd frames.** Only 8 of 58 test videos had every crop right. The worst, V_DRONE_019 and V_DRONE_050, had 22 and 22 of 33 crops wrong, with wrong frames spread across the whole clip (indices 0, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75 and on). Errors come in whole-clip runs rather than isolated frames.

5. **Two scenes decide the drone result.** The 32 drone test videos come from 2 scene clusters. Drone crops wrong: 47.8% in the cluster with 27 videos (6 close, 15 medium, 6 distant videos) and 84.8% in the cluster with 5 videos (0 close, 1 medium, 4 distant videos). Scene and distance cannot be separated here: the worse scene is mostly distant clips.

6. **Visible drones are missed on plain sky (looked at by eye, local only).** In a random sample of 8 drone crops called neither, several show a clearly visible quadcopter shape on plain sky, not just a blur. Nothing was saved. That fits the model leaning on the scene: most of the background crops it learned as neither are plain sky (checked by eye on a sample).

7. **Brighter and plainer crops fail more.** For bird and drone crops, wrong share by brightness third: 33.0% (darkest), 39.7%, 61.9% (brightest). By texture third (pixel standard deviation): 50.2% (plainest), 61.6%, 22.9% (most texture). These are associations only. Brightness and texture also track scene and distance, so this does not show that lighting causes the errors.

8. **Background is easy, so neither looks better than it is.** 2 of 629 background crops were wrong (0.3%). Plain sky is easy to call neither without learning anything about objects.

9. **Look-alikes are confused with the targets.** 50 of 213 airplane crops (23.5%) were called bird or drone, and 18 of 83 helicopter crops (21.7%) were called drone. Airplane crops wrong by distance: 53.5% close (2 videos), 19.1% medium, 14.0% distant. In the sample I looked at, a large, clear airplane was called bird. Only 2 close airplane videos and 4 helicopter videos are in the test set.

10. **High precision costs most of the recall.** Bird alert at P(bird) >= 0.5: precision 0.618, recall 0.768. At the threshold chosen on validation for precision >= 0.90 (0.9936): precision 0.979, recall 0.352, 96 alerts. The firmware's default of 0.85 gave precision 0.801 and recall 0.588. The test set is small, so each figure has a wide interval (see the README).
